# NAMO region-opening literature map and reading queue

*Compiled 2026-07-17; focused domain-literature audit updated 2026-08-04. Entries marked PDF-audited were checked against their full texts. For metadata-checked entries, the bibliographic identity is recorded but the descriptive claim is not automatically cite-ready: verify it against the source before putting it in the manuscript.*

**The problem these serve:** ~50–80 candidate pushes on one given object. The simulator executes any candidate in ~1s and says exactly whether it opened the region, so the model is a ranker that chooses the order in which pushes are tried. The objective is `E[#simulator calls until a success]`.

This file has two layers: the first two sections tell you what to read and how the manuscript uses it; the remaining sections are the complete topic-organized library. `read` records your reading progress, `role` records why the manuscript needs the paper, and `verification` records how deeply its citation and claims have been checked. `unread` means only “not recorded as read here,” not that nobody has inspected the source. Role precedence is: `baseline` for a comparison method or source of one, `must-cite` for central non-baseline framing, and `context` for optional background; a baseline paper is still something the manuscript should cite.

## Method queue

**Recommended order:** `weitzman` → `contextual-pandora` → `wells-feasibility` → `garrett-rank` → `chrestien-ranking` → `phs-guarantees` → `ferber-boot` → `nnet-survival` → `deephit` → `uct` → `adaptive-submodularity`. This moves from optimal candidate ordering, to learning a search heuristic, to learning from censored search experience, and finally to changing the search policy after verified failures.

### Ordering candidates under a perfect, expensive verifier

*This is the objective, and it has a name and a 1979 solution. None of it was in the repo.*

- `weitzman` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Weitzman — Optimal Search for the Best Alternative** (Econometrica
      47(3):641–654, 1979, doi:10.2307/1910412). *Pandora's Box.* N alternatives, each costs `c`
      to open, opening reveals the true value exactly, you stop when satisfied. Optimal rule is an
      index: a **reservation value** per box, open in that order. **With uniform costs — every push
      is ~1s — it collapses to: sort by success probability, descending.** This is the theory of
      the thing the ranker does, and it is 47 years old.
      *The Econometrica version is paywalled everywhere — Unpaywall, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar
      and CORE all agree no published version is deposited anywhere. The copy here is the same
      paper as **MIT Energy Laboratory Report MIT-EL-78-008** (May 1978), written for the US
      Department of Energy under contract EX-76-A-01-2295. Pagination is `-1-` to `-21-`, not
      641–654, so cite the Econometrica version and read this one.*
      *Two traps if you re-fetch it: `scholar.harvard.edu/files/weitzman/...` ranks first in
      search and returns a 403 Akamai page; and MIT DSpace throttles with **HTTP 202 and zero
      bytes** rather than an error, so a naive fetch silently gets nothing.*
- `contextual-pandora` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Atsidakou, Caramanis, Gergatsouli, Papadigenopoulos, Tzamos —
      Contextual Pandora's Box** (AAAI 2024, arXiv:2205.13114). Weitzman when the distributions
      are unknown and must be learned from context — i.e. when a network estimates the
      probabilities rather than an oracle handing them over.
- `pandora-correlations` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Gergatsouli & Tzamos — Weitzman's Rule for Pandora's Box with
      Correlations** (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2301.13534). The index rule assumes independent boxes.
      Pushes on the same object are not independent. This is what breaks and what survives.
- `pandora-nonobligatory` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Fu, Li, Liu — Pandora Box Problem with Nonobligatory
      Inspection** (STOC 2023, arXiv:2207.09545). When you may commit to a box without paying to
      inspect it. Hardness plus an approximation scheme.
- `adaptive-submodularity` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Golovin & Krause — Adaptive Submodularity** (JAIR 2011,
      arXiv:1003.3967). The framework for "choose what to inspect next given what the last
      inspections revealed" — i.e. re-ranking after each failed push, if failures are informative.
- `online-mssc-pandora` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Gergatsouli & Tzamos — Online Learning for Min Sum Set Cover and
      Pandora's Box** (ICML 2022, arXiv:2202.04870). Min-sum set cover is exactly "order things to
      minimise expected position of the first hit", learned online.
- `best-arm-fixed-confidence` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Garivier & Kaufmann — Optimal Best Arm Identification with
      Fixed Confidence** (COLT 2016, arXiv:1602.04589). The bandit view: pure exploration, sample
      complexity lower bounds. Adjacent rather than exact — you stop at a *success*, not at
      confidence about the *best* arm — but it's the right neighbouring theory.

**The one thing this literature says that the repo does not:** sorting by `p̂/c` is Bayes-optimal
*only when the estimates are real conditional probabilities*. For 1-push that costs nothing —
order is invariant under any monotone transform, so "we need order, not calibration" is exactly
right. For 2-push it is not: comparing a direct push at `p=0.3` against a setup-then-finish at
`0.5 × 0.6 = 0.3` needs the numbers, not the ranking. The literature's form is a shallow
prerequisite tree with the finish conditioned on the realised setup state.

### Search-guided value learning under a perfect verifier

- `htps` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Lample et al. — HyperTree Proof Search for Neural Theorem Proving**
      (NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2205.11491). Neural theorem proving with a critic trained on its own

- `eg-mcts` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Hong et al. — Retrosynthetic Planning with Experience-Guided MCTS**

      should reflect the actual decomposition situation rather than a penalty.
- `stahlberg-unsolvability` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Ståhlberg, Francès, Seipp — Learning Generalized Unsolvability
      Heuristics for Classical Planning** (IJCAI 2021, distinguished paper). Labels training data
      by exhaustively exploring small instances, then generalises to large ones.
- `li-dantam-infeasibility` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Li & Dantam — Learning Proofs of Motion Planning
      Infeasibility** (RSS 2021; journal version IJRR 2023). Constructs infeasibility proofs
      rather than inferring them from a timeout.
- `wells-feasibility` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Wells, Dantam, Shrivastava, Kavraki — Learning Feasibility for Task
      and Motion Planning in Tabletop Environments** (RA-L 4(2):1255–1262, 2019). A learned
      feasibility classifier used to order a TAMP search, in tabletop. Handles "planner timeout ≠
      infeasible" explicitly and keeps probabilistic completeness when the classifier is wrong.

### Labels from search: censoring, and where heuristic-learning gets its targets

*Added 2026-07-18 (Opus, from the beast-0 post-mortem). The finding these serve: our depth-k labeling wrote "didn't find an opening within k" as a hard 0 — but that's a right-censored observation ("V ≤ γ^k"), not a value; 91% of the beast-0 loss was such false zeros, and the model regressed on depth-1 skill it already had. Two questions: (a) the principled loss for exact-labels-plus-ceilings on a categorical value head; (b) how the learning-for-search field generates labels at all — most of it appears to train on solved instances only and never proves negatives. **Citations below are from model memory, NOT yet PDF-audited** unless marked otherwise — the three research-agent sweeps have since landed and the marked entries were verified against primary sources; verify unmarked ids on fetch.*

*The combinatorial-search sweep's one-line verdict: NOBODY in this literature exhaustively proves negatives — the field trains on solved instances only (negatives come free from the loss), manufactures exact labels (backward generation, hindsight relabeling), or gives unresolved cases a soft finite proxy; and for minimize-expansions, ORDERING losses beat value regression (even true optimal cost-to-go fails the ordering property). The four entries below carry that verdict.*

- `garrett-rank` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `PDF-audited` — **Garrett, Kaelbling, Lozano-Pérez — Learning to Rank for Synthesizing Planning Heuristics** (IJCAI 2016, arXiv:1608.01302). *PDF-audited 2026-07-24 (in `papers/1608.01302`).* The earlier precedent for `chrestien-ranking`'s thesis: train the heuristic with a ranking loss (RankSVM) because GBFS only consumes the ORDER; beats regression on IPC learning-track domains. *A web sweep attributed this to "Rosman & Ramamoorthy" — fabricated; title page says Garrett/Kaelbling/Lozano-Pérez.*
- `chrestien-ranking` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `PDF-audited` — **Chrestien, Pevný, Edelkamp, Komenda — Optimize Planning Heuristics to Rank, not to Estimate Cost-to-Goal** (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2310.19463). *The sharpest result for us, audited:* minimal-expansion search is characterized by an ORDERING condition; even the TRUE h\* fails it (worked counterexample); ranking losses beat L2-to-h\* in 8/8 domains; the negatives are free — one-hop off-path siblings of solution-path states. Theory for the repo's own "we need the right ORDER, not calibrated probabilities" line.
- `minimo` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `PDF-audited` — **Poesia et al. — MINIMO: Learning Formal Mathematics from Intrinsic Motivation** (NeurIPS 2024). *Audited.* The search-tree analog of HER: failed best-first proof searches are mined for subtrees that accidentally proved something else → exact positives from failures. Their ablation: solved-only WITHOUT hindsight relabeling starves the loop (~10-20% of attempts yield signal) — the fix was more exact positives from failures, not negatives. Maps directly to mining our failed sweeps for pushes that opened a *different* region pair. *arXiv:2407.00695 — the arXiv title is "Learning Formal Mathematics From Intrinsic Motivation", no "MINIMO:" prefix; MINIMO is the system name.*
- `ferber-boot` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `PDF-audited` — **Ferber, Geißer, Trevizan, Helmert, Hoffmann — Neural Network Heuristic Functions for Classical Planning: Bootstrapping and Comparison to Other
      Methods** (ICAPS 2022, pp. 583–587). *Audited.* Short paper, 5 pages. The cleanest documented answer to censored labels in planning: timed-out states get a FINITE soft proxy (expansions-until-timeout), tested and preferred over both a big constant penalty and discarding; and self-bootstrap beats teacher imitation once the teacher hits its ceiling.
- `lts-cm` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Orseau, Hutter, Lelis — Levin Tree Search with Context Models** (IJCAI 2023, arXiv:2305.16945). The expansions-bound Σd/π used ITSELF as a convex loss — flips the 5×5 sliding puzzle from 0.9% to 100% solved with the same objective where neural policy-only collapsed. The strongest version of "the training loss should literally be the deploy metric." NB: LevinTS is parked in this repo as "not properly tested," not falsified.

- `nnet-survival` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `PDF-audited` — **Gensheimer & Narasimhan — A scalable discrete-time survival model for neural networks** (PeerJ 2019, arXiv:1805.00917). The standard ML treatment of right-censored targets with a discrete/categorical head: per-interval hazards trained with cross-entropy, censored cases contribute `-log P(survive past k)` — i.e. exactly "penalize only mass above the ceiling." The direct template for our censored value loss. *Audited 2026-07-18 against the PMC full text: censored obs "receives credit for surviving through the censoring interval but no likelihood component for an actual event" — the claimed form is verbatim there.*
- `deephit` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Lee, Zame, Yoon, van der Schaar — DeepHit: A Deep Learning Approach to Survival Analysis with Competing Risks** (AAAI 2018). *The closest single precedent to our whole design:* parameterizes the discrete PMF over time-bins directly with a softmax (= our 51-bin head), trains with censored NLL (uncensored → `-log PMF@event`; censored → `-log CDF` past the censor point) PLUS a pairwise ranking loss restricted to pairs whose order is *provably known under censoring* — the exact "certain-pairs ranking aux" we sketched. Read §loss first.
- `elkan-noto-pu` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Elkan & Noto — Learning Classifiers from Only Positive and Unlabeled Data** (KDD 2008). The other frame for "didn't find ≠ negative": our dead-within-k cells are unlabeled-not-negative. The classic PU correction; read with `nnpu`.
- `nnpu` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Kiryo, Niu, du Plessis, Sugiyama — Positive-Unlabeled Learning with Non-Negative Risk Estimators** (NeurIPS 2017, arXiv:1703.00593). The modern deep-learning-safe PU risk estimator (the naive one goes negative and overfits).
- `time-limits-rl` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Pardo, Tavakoli, Levdik, Kormushev — Time Limits in Reinforcement Learning** (ICML 2018, arXiv:1712.00378). Timeout-truncated returns are censored returns; bootstrapping-past-the-limit vs treating timeout as terminal. Same bug class as ours, RL dialect.
- `bootstrap-heuristics` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Jabbari Arfaee, Zilles, Holte — Learning Heuristic Functions for Large State Spaces** (AIJ 175(16-17):2075–2098, 2011). The bootstrap procedure: solve what you can, train ONLY on solved instances, use the better heuristic to solve more. Trains on no negatives at all — the direct precedent for our ladder, minus our exhaustive dead-proving.
- `deepcubea` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Agostinelli, McAleer, Shmakov, Baldi — Solving the Rubik's Cube with Deep Reinforcement Learning and Search** (Nature Machine Intelligence 1:356–363, 2019). Sidesteps censoring entirely: generate states BACKWARD from the goal so distance-to-goal is known by construction. Worth asking how far the trick carries when the generator owns scene construction (ours does).
      *No arXiv version. arXiv:1805.07470 is a **different, earlier** paper ("Solving the Rubik's
      Cube Without Human Knowledge", McAleer first author) — do not substitute it. The canonical
      `deepcube.igb.uci.edu` mirror is dead; copy here is from cse.sc.edu.*
- `phs-guarantees` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Orseau & Lelis — Policy-Guided Heuristic Search with Guarantees** (AAAI 2021, arXiv:2103.11505). Learns a policy whose loss directly bounds EXPANSIONS-TO-SOLUTION — literally our success metric, and it needs only solution trajectories, no negatives. NB: LevinTS is parked in this repo as "not properly tested," not falsified.
- `exit` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Anthony, Tian, Barber — Thinking Fast and Slow with Deep Learning and Tree Search** (NeurIPS 2017, arXiv:1705.08439). Expert Iteration — the search-generates-labels loop the whole curriculum is an instance of; read for what they feed back (search-improved targets, not proofs of deadness).
- `alphazero` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Silver et al. — A general reinforcement learning algorithm that masters chess, shogi and Go through self-play** (Science 362(6419):1140–1144, 2018; preprint arXiv:1712.01815 "Mastering Chess and Shogi by Self-Play…"). The canonical instance of `exit`'s loop at scale: search produces the targets, the net produces the prior that makes the next search cheaper. Two things to read for us, not the self-play story — (a) PUCT: the learned prior enters as a *prior over which child to try first*, decayed by visit count, exactly the ranker's job here; (b) the value target is the game outcome, i.e. an exact verified label, never a censored "didn't find one in k" — so it says nothing about our false-zero problem and shouldn't be cited for it. Method detail is thinner here than in the sibling **AlphaGo Zero — Mastering the game of Go without human knowledge** (Nature 550:354–359, 2017); read that one for the actual training/loss spec. *Citations from memory, NOT PDF-audited.*
- `her` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Andrychowicz et al. — Hindsight Experience Replay** (NeurIPS 2017, arXiv:1707.01495). Failed episodes relabeled as successes for the goals they DID reach — exact labels extracted from failures. The search-tree analog of mining our failed sweeps for free positives.
- `learn-to-branch` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Gasse, Chételat, Ferroni, Charlin, Lodi — Exact Combinatorial Optimization with Graph Convolutional Neural Networks** (NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1906.01629). Learning to rank branching candidates by imitating an expensive oracle (strong branching) — the OR field's version of sim-verified label generation, trained as ranking not regression.
- `stop-regressing` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Farebrother et al. — Stop Regressing: Training Value Functions via Classification for Scalable Deep RL** (ICML 2024, arXiv:2403.03950). The HL-Gauss head we already use; re-read §method for how the categorical form composes with a censored (cumulative-mass) likelihood rather than a point target.

### Adapting the search when the heuristic misleads (failure-discount lineage)

*Added 2026-07-24, motivated by the queue-suppression trace (EXP-2026-07-21 card): 63–77% of 2push search cost is depth-2 children of wrong roots flooding the static queue above the true setup. The proposed fix — per-board credibility demoted by verified failures — has ancestors in four separate communities. Citations web-verified 2026-07-24 (Haiku sweep, links checked), NOT PDF-audited. The sweep found NO published match for the exact mechanism (learned ranker + perfect verifier + sibling demotion on failure) — closest strands below; `adaptive-submodularity` above is the theory anchor for "failures are informative re-ranking evidence." Added PNS and df-pn 2026-07-26: these are the classical exact treatment of the same "proof/disproof credibility" quantity we soft-weight with verified failures; PNS/df-pn solve AND/OR trees with a perfect verifier, the exact shape of our problem.*

- `pns` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Allis, van der Meulen, van den Herik — Proof-Number Search** (Artificial Intelligence 66(1):91–124, 1994, doi:10.1016/0004-3702(94)90004-3). The classical exact treatment of AND/OR tree search with a **perfect verifier**: proof and disproof numbers track minimum changes needed to prove/disprove a node, solving the same "binary feasibility + free verifier" problem our suppression trace measures. Lineage anchor for failure-driven heuristic demotion.
- `df-pn` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Nagai — Df-pn Algorithm for Searching AND/OR Trees and Its Applications** (PhD thesis, University of Tokyo, 2002). Depth-first reformulation of PNS with transposition tables and iterative deepening: the practical, memory-efficient variant. Introduced λ-search for handling cycles; extended to Tsume-Go and Tsume-Shogi where contact/feasibility rules break straightforward minimax.
- `koopman-search` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Koopman — Search and Screening: General Principles with Historical Applications** (OEG Report 56, 1946; republished MORS 1999). Bayesian search theory: failed looks in a box shift posterior mass away from it; allocate next look by posterior × detection probability. The exact math of our board demotion (boards = boxes, sims = looks); lineage runs through the USS Scorpion and MH370 searches.
- `gbfs-behaviour` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Heusner, Keller, Helmert — Understanding the Search Behaviour of Greedy Best-First Search** (SoCS 2017). Formalizes how GBFS gets trapped in misleading heuristic regions (high-water-mark benches) — the named pathology our suppression trace measured with a verifier.
- `gbfs-exploration` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Valenzano, Sturtevant, Schaeffer, Xie — A Comparison of Knowledge-Based GBFS Enhancements and Knowledge-Free Exploration** (ICAPS 2014). ε-greedy node selection as the cure for heuristic error in GBFS. *Sweep correction: venue is ICAPS, not SoCS.*
- `type-gbfs` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Xie, Müller, Holte, Imai — Type-Based Exploration with Multiple Search Queues for Satisficing Planning** (AAAI 2014). Alternating typed queues so one misleading region can't monopolize expansion — the alternation flavor of anti-camping.
- `lds` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Harvey & Ginsberg — Limited Discrepancy Search** (IJCAI 1995). Budget the number of times you disobey the heuristic; trust-but-bound. Closest classic in spirit to taxing (not obeying, not discarding) an unreliable ranking.
- `chaff-vsids` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Moskewicz, Madigan, Zhao, Zhang, Malik — Chaff: Engineering an Efficient SAT Solver** (DAC 2001). VSIDS: the solver's own conflicts (failures) continuously re-weight its branching heuristic, with decay. Failure-driven heuristic adaptation at industrial scale.
- `dirt` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Littlefield & Bekris — Efficient and Asymptotically Optimal Kinodynamic Motion Planning via Dominance-Informed Regions** (IROS 2018). Own lab. Heuristic-guided single-query planner that demotes a node after each selection so the heuristic can't camp — our regime, count-weighted where ours is verifier-evidence-weighted.
- `uct` · read `unread` · role `baseline` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Kocsis & Szepesvári — Bandit Based Monte-Carlo Planning** (ECML 2006). UCT: visit-count exploration + outcome backup. Our mechanism is the backup step in the deterministic perfect-verifier limit (no revisits, so the UCB term degenerates); PUCT (Silver et al., AlphaGo/AlphaZero) adds the learned prior, as we do.
- `rtaa-star` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Koenig & Likhachev — Real-Time Adaptive A\*** (AAMAS 2006). The heuristic-learns-online family: exact admissibility-preserving h-updates from search experience. Contrast, not ancestor — our q has no cost algebra to correct, so our update is Bayesian trust, not arithmetic.

### Very close related work

*Added 2026-08-02 after tracing the region-opening formulation through the NAMO literature. The classical name for our local task is a **keyhole**: manipulate one or more objects to connect two disconnected free-space components. Our 1-push and 2-push episodes are both single-object keyholes—classical `LP1` counts objects moved, not pushes. The closest classical routine is `Manip-Search(C_robot, C_goal, O)`; our contribution replaces its uninformed/geometric action ordering with a learned controller-aware ranker and measures simulator calls to the first verified opening.*

**Manuscript-only shortcut:** `stilman-kuffner` → `levihn-opening-detection` → `levihn-hierarchical-namo`/`levihn-continuous-namo` → `scholz-learned-dynamics` → `yao-local-namo` → `de-luca-push-planner` → `dai-adaptive-pushing`/`yang-namo-hrl` → `bench-push`; use the other entries to understand assumptions, guarantees, perception, and deployment rather than to define the central claim.

- `stilman-kuffner` · read `unread` · role `baseline` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Stilman & Kuffner — Navigation Among Movable Obstacles: Real-Time Reasoning in Complex Environments** (IJHR 2(4):479–503, 2005). The exact problem ancestor: defines a keyhole as connecting disconnected free-space components by moving objects, and `Manip-Search(C1,C2,O)` as bounded search over reachable contacts and manipulation actions for one object. No learned push ordering.
- `levihn-opening-detection` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Levihn & Stilman — Efficient Opening Detection** (Georgia Tech Technical Report GT-GOLEM-2011-004, 2011). The exact ancestor of our success verifier: detects whether moving an arbitrarily shaped obstacle creates a new free-space connection and argues that restricting manipulation reasoning to opening-producing moves yields large NAMO speedups. Our wavefront-based verifier is a different implementation, but the paper establishes that opening detection—not object displacement by itself—is the task-level event that matters.
- `stilman-artificial-constraints` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `PDF-audited` — **Stilman & Kuffner — Planning Among Movable Obstacles with Artificial Constraints** (WAFR 2006; IJRR 27(11–12):1295–1307, 2008). The direct sequel to `stilman-kuffner`, and the paper that attacks the object coupling the 2005 planner left open. Replaces `LP_k C_m` with a two-axis taxonomy — `L_k`/`NL` (objects per keyhole vs. interaction between keyholes) × `M`/`NM_i` (monotone = each object moved once) — and operates in `L_k M`. Mechanism: reverse-time search in which each *future* Transit/Transfer swept volume becomes a hard artificial constraint on *earlier* object placements, i.e. the classical non-learned way to prune the candidate set before testing it rather than ranking and testing forward. Solves scenes the 2005 planner cannot (its Fig. 1, 2, 4), but explicitly forfeits `L1` completeness to do it: committing to Transit paths early is what buys the speed and what loses the remote Transit paths `[1]` would find. Table 1: 4–9 objects, 2–6 transferred, 0.05–2.08s on a Pentium M 1.6GHz. *PDF-audited 2026-08-04 against the CMU WAFR copy (publications.ri.cmu.edu, stilman_michael_2006_2); taxonomy, Table 1 and the completeness caveat are verbatim there.*
- `nieuwenhuisen-action-tree` · read `unread` · role `baseline` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Nieuwenhuisen, van der Stappen, Overmars — An Effective Framework for Path Planning Amidst Movable Obstacles** (WAFR 2006; Algorithmic Foundations of Robotics VII, pp. 87–102). A probabilistically complete action tree over alternating navigation and manipulation actions, accelerated by hand-designed probability propagation and practical heuristics. It covers broader multi-object NAMO with idealized push/pull manipulation; for our local problem it is a classical heuristic-search baseline family, not a controller-realistic competitor.
- `vandenberg-complete-namo` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **van den Berg, Stilman, Kuffner, Lin, Manocha — Path Planning Among Movable Obstacles: A Probabilistically Complete Approach** (WAFR 2008; Algorithmic Foundations of Robotics VIII, pp. 599–614, 2009). Decouples robot motion from obstacle movement and grows a random tree over world states using straight-line obstacle actions while retaining probabilistic completeness. Read it to state exactly what our finite primitive set gives up: we deliberately trade continuous-action completeness for cheap, repeatable controller execution and learn which discretized actions to test first.
- `moghaddam-recursive-namo` · read `unread` · role `baseline` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Moghaddam & Masehian — Planning Robot Navigation Among Movable Obstacles (NAMO) Through a Recursive Approach** (Journal of Intelligent & Robotic Systems 83:603–634, 2016). Recursively decomposes full NAMO into one-object subproblems and uses visibility graphs plus penetration depth to propose object translations. It handles linear/nonlinear and monotone/non-monotone cases under strong geometric assumptions—push/pull without rotation or dynamics—and is useful as a modern geometric baseline concept, not a direct physics baseline.
- `stilman-rsc` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `PDF-audited` — **Stilman, Schamburek, Kuffner, Asfour — Manipulation Planning Among Movable Obstacles** (ICRA 2007). RSC (`ResolveSpatialConstraints`): the sampling-based lift of `stilman-artificial-constraints` off the plane into full manipulator C-space. Still monotone — the paper's own future work names inter-object motion dependence as unhandled. Simulated 3-link planar arm and an 8-DOF mobile manipulator based on the 6-DOF PUMA; workshop scene of 28 objects, 16 movable, doors constrained to fixed axes. Has the experimental rigor the 2005 paper lacks: 100 trials per scenario, `t_total` 7.15–13.26s, 3.0–4.6 objects moved, plus a per-subtask cost breakdown (RRT 46.7%, swept volumes 22.9%, placement 11.8%). *PDF-audited 2026-08-04 against golems.org/papers/StilmanICRA07-manipulation-movable.pdf. NB: RSC itself is simulation-only (PUMA/planar-arm, no humanoid) — the HRP-2 hardware work is the separate `stilman-namo-hrp2` entry, don't conflate them.*
- `stilman-namo-hrp2` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `PDF-audited` — **Stilman, Nishiwaki, Kagami, Kuffner — Planning and Executing Navigation Among Movable Obstacles** (IROS 2006; Advanced Robotics 21(14), 2007). The only hardware entry in this lineage: the `stilman-kuffner` planner closed onto a real HRP-2 humanoid in a 25m² area, which it perceives and models itself (vision tracking + joint encoders + four six-axis force sensors; model fitting to 0.125m³ voxels), then executes as walking + *compliant* manipulation with online feedback — wrist force sensors driving a linear spring-damper, the physical realization of the spring-damper arm constraint that was only simulated in the 2005 paper. Table I: planning is negligible against execution (real run — NAMO 0.06s, navigation 0.09s, execution **63.0s**; simulated — 0.13s / 0.93s / 153.0s); simulated worlds up to 10 movable obstacles. Read for what breaks between plan and execution, not for search cost. *PDF-audited 2026-08-04 against kuffner.org/james/papers/NAMO_plan_exec_iros2006.pdf.*
- `levihn-hierarchical-namo` · read `unread` · role `baseline` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Levihn, Scholz, Stilman — Hierarchical Decision Theoretic Planning for Navigation Among Movable Obstacles** (WAFR 2012; Algorithmic Foundations of Robotics X, 2013, pp. 19–35). Two-level NAMO-MDP: high-level actions open a neighbouring free-space region through a chosen obstacle; low-level MCTS/sparse sampling searches primitive object manipulations under uncertain outcomes. Direct precedent for a tree-search baseline, though its simulated primitives are axis-aligned and its purpose is uncertainty-aware policy construction rather than minimum simulator calls to first success.
- `levihn-continuous-namo` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Levihn, Scholz, Stilman — Planning with Movable Obstacles in Continuous Environments with Uncertain Dynamics** (ICRA 2013, pp. 3832–3838). The continuous-action successor to `levihn-hierarchical-namo`: keeps the neighbouring-region MDP but replaces discretized low-level MCTS with a kinodynamic RRT that searches manipulation controls while Monte Carlo sampling uncertain object-dynamics models in Box2D. It is a direct predecessor for simulator-guided `Manip-Search` with realistic controllers, but optimizes uncertainty-aware NAMO decisions rather than learning an amortized cross-scene ranking that minimizes simulator calls.
- `scholz-object-models` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Scholz, Levihn, Isbell, Christensen, Stilman — Learning Non-Holonomic Object Models for Mobile Manipulation** (ICRA 2015, pp. 5531–5536). Learns structured dynamics models for carts and tables from robot interaction; it is the model-identification component used by `scholz-learned-dynamics`, not an opening planner by itself.
- `scholz-learned-dynamics` · read `unread` · role `baseline` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Scholz, Jindal, Levihn, Isbell, Christensen — Navigation Among Movable Obstacles with Learned Dynamic Constraints** (IROS 2016, pp. 3706–3713). Uses the same neighbouring-region clearing abstraction and evaluates grasp angle, object velocity, and trajectory length in simulation. Crucial correction: the object dynamics are learned, but the dynamics-conditioned action sampler is a hand-written decision tree over four model classes; the authors explicitly call it an optional heuristic that trades completeness for computation time. That sampler is therefore a strong non-neural baseline for our learned action ranker.
- `yao-local-namo` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Yao, Modugno, Delfaki, Liu, Stoyanov, Kanoulas — Local Path Planning among Pushable Objects based on Reinforcement Learning** (arXiv:2303.02407, 2023; revised 2024). Trains an A2C policy in physics simulation for local navigation through confined scenes with pushable objects and transfers it to a quadruped. It is the closest task-level learned system, but it does not isolate a one-object keyhole verifier or rank a fixed candidate set: it evaluates end-to-end robot goal-reaching under continuous control.
- `emeli-joint-push` · read `unread` · role `baseline` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Emeli & Cosgun — Joint Path and Push Planning Among Movable Obstacles** (arXiv:2010.14733, 2020). Interleaves global path selection with physics-based means-end search for sequences of linear pushes that clear robot-width space. Very close physically, but uses an RRT/geometric planning heuristic rather than a learned push ranker.
- `camp-contact-aware` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Wang, Wang, Gao, Shen — Contact-Aware Motion Planning Among Movable Objects** (arXiv:2502.03317, 2025). CAMP places robot–object contact, friction, and non-penetration directly into continuous trajectory optimization as complementarity constraints, allowing the optimizer to choose when and how to push without a preset contact-mode sequence. It is the cleanest optimization-based counterpoint to our primitives: richer continuous actions and an explicit mechanics objective versus a finite executable action set and a learned order under a binary opening verifier.
- `weeda-svg-mppi` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Weeda, Bakker, Chen, Alonso-Mora — Pushing Through Clutter With Movability Awareness of Blocking Obstacles** (ICRA 2025, arXiv:2502.20106). Adds passage nodes to connect otherwise disconnected areas and uses GPU-parallel physics-rollout MPPI to push through them while accounting for continuous movability. A strong contemporary physics-aware comparison, but trajectory optimization rather than discrete setup/finish ranking.
- `de-luca-push-planner` · read `unread` · role `baseline` · verification `metadata-checked` — **De Luca, Muratore, Tsagarakis — A Hierarchical Simulation-Based Push Planner for Autonomous Recovery in Navigation-Blocked Scenarios of Mobile Robots** (Robotics and Autonomous Systems 184:104867, 2025). Searches multi-push object trajectories with RRT-like global planning and parallel MuJoCo rollouts, then executes on RELAX and CENTAURO. This is the closest modern non-learning external comparison to our full search stack: both use a simulator as an online action-outcome oracle, but it samples continuous end-effector pushes toward a chosen object pose while we rank a fixed controller-grounded set by opening value.
- `dai-adaptive-pushing` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Dai, Liu, Sreenath, Li, Hollis — Interactive Navigation With Adaptive Non-Prehensile Mobile Manipulation** (RA-L 11(2):1154–1161, 2026; arXiv:2410.13418). Learns an adaptive SE(2) object-dynamics representation from interaction and places it inside MPPI on the balancing Shmoobot. It is a strong current continuous-control comparison for unknown dynamics; it learns transition prediction rather than the search ordering of verified opening attempts.
- `bejjani-learned-rhp` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Bejjani, Papallas, Leonetti, Dogar — Planning with a Receding Horizon for Manipulation in Clutter Using a Learned Value Function** (Humanoids 2018, arXiv:1803.08100). Closest methodological predecessor: learns an action-value heuristic from sampling-based planner solutions, improves it with RL, and uses it to guide closed-loop physics-based pushing search. Its goal is pushing a target object to a target region in tabletop clutter, not opening a navigation keyhole.
- `namo-llm` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Zhang & Kantaros — NAMO-LLM: Efficient Navigation Among Movable Obstacles with Large Language Model Guidance** (RA-L 2025, arXiv:2505.04141). Same search-efficiency thesis at a different abstraction: non-uniform LLM guidance chooses which object and placement direction to sample in a NAMO tree. It assumes holonomic grasp-and-relocate actions, not contact-rich push primitives; Case Study V compares RandomTree and structured NAMO-SA.

### Broader NAMO lineage, perception and deployment

*A focused forward-citation and terminology audit through 2026-08-04 checked the Stilman keyhole work, Levihn's hierarchical NAMO, Scholz's learned-dynamics NAMO, and Yao's local RL paper against newer “interactive navigation” work. It added Interactive-FAR, tactile negotiation, HERO, and CoINS below; this is a focused audit, not a claim that every citation to those anchors has been enumerated.*

- `wu-unknown-namo` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Wu, Levihn, Stilman — Navigation Among Movable Obstacles in Unknown Environments** (IROS 2010, pp. 1433–1438). Extends NAMO to partial maps and interaction-based movability discovery while reducing planning relative to direct world-state search. Read for replanning and information assumptions; our canonical local problem starts after the relevant blocker and regions are already identified.
- `levihn-locally-optimal-namo` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Levihn, Stilman, Christensen — Locally Optimal Navigation Among Movable Obstacles in Unknown Environments** (Humanoids 2014, pp. 86–91). Formalizes a locally optimal online NAMO policy for unknown scenes under single-object-move assumptions. It matters because later S-NAMO and deployment work builds on this object-selection/planning loop, whereas our work isolates and learns the low-level keyhole-action order inside that loop.
- `castaman-tree-namo` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Castaman, Tosello, Pagello — A Sampling-Based Tree Planner for Navigation Among Movable Obstacles** (ISR 2016). Combines A* with a sampling-based tree and hand-designed relocation preferences, including ordering placements behind the robot and escalating after failed displacement. Useful evidence that action/placement ordering has long been recognized as decisive, even though the heuristic is geometric and the manipulation model differs.
- `interactive-far` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **He, Chen, Wang, Zhang, Fermüller, Aloimonos — Interactive-FAR: Interactive, Fast and Adaptable Routing for Navigation Among Movable Obstacles in Complex Unknown Environments** (IROS 2024, pp. 5402–5409; arXiv:2404.07447). Maintains a dynamic directed visibility graph, attempts obstacle interactions during global routing, and updates obstacle affordances from online sensory feedback. It is a fast full-system planner for unknown environments, so it belongs above our local opener and supplies modern deployment context rather than a push-ranking baseline.
- `s-namo-analysis` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Renault, Saraydaryan, Simonin — Towards S-NAMO: Socially-Aware Navigation Among Movable Obstacles** (arXiv:1909.10809, 2019). Contains an unusually useful analysis table of the NAMO literature by world representation, cost/optimality, manipulation assumptions, and planner. Its own contribution adds social movability and taboo-placement costs; read the survey section to audit coverage and the method section only if discussing the downstream consequences of where an opened obstacle is left.
- `ellis-visual-namo` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Ellis, Zhang, Stoyanov, Kanoulas — Navigation Among Movable Obstacles With Object Localization Using Photorealistic Simulation** (IROS 2022, pp. 1711–1716). Integrates visual object localization, interactive movability detection, graph planning, pushing, localization, and mapping in Isaac Sim and on a mobile robot. It is deployment evidence for the larger NAMO pipeline around our deliberately isolated region-opening module.
- `muguira-vanamo` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Muguira-Iturralde, Curtis, Du, Kaelbling, Lozano-Pérez — Visibility-Aware Navigation Among Movable Obstacles** (ICRA 2023, pp. 10083–10089; arXiv:2212.02671). LaMB backchains through visibility and manipulation subgoals in 3D partially observed scenes and explicitly describes previous NAMO as keyhole search over connected configuration-space regions. This establishes where our formulation fits and what it omits: discovering safe views and affordances before local opening search.
- `ellis-storage-zones` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Ellis, Hadjivelichkov, Modugno, Stoyanov, Kanoulas — Navigation Among Movable Obstacles via Multi-Object Pushing Into Storage Zones** (IEEE Access 11:3174–3183, 2023). Plans axis-aligned multi-push routes that clear a path while leaving objects in predefined storage zones. It addresses the downstream placement/social-cost question that our binary opening objective intentionally ignores.
- `armleder-tactile-namo` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Armleder, Dean-Leon, Bergner, Guadarrama Olvera, Cheng — Tactile-Based Negotiation of Unknown Objects during Navigation in Unstructured Environments with Movable Obstacles** (Advanced Intelligent Systems 6(3):2300621, 2024). Uses proximity and tactile skin feedback to test whether an unknown obstacle is movable, then plans either clearance or avoidance and executes compliant pushing on the TOMM mobile manipulator. Read it for active movability verification, failure recovery, and real-robot assumptions around the deterministic local problem we currently study.
- `namounc` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Zhang, Lucet, Alexandre dit Sandretto, Chen, Filliat — NAMOUnc: Navigation Among Movable Obstacles With Decision Making on Uncertainty Interval** (ICINCO 2025; arXiv:2509.12723). Estimates intervals for observation, model, action, and partial-observability uncertainty, then chooses whether to remove or bypass an obstacle by time-cost utility. It operates above our local opener, but its treatment of action failure is directly relevant when moving from deterministic simulation ranking to hardware decisions.
- `bridge-3d-namo` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Kalluraya & Kantaros — Making Infeasible Tasks Feasible: Planning to Reconfigure Disconnected 3D Environments With Movable Objects** (arXiv:2601.02645, 2026). BRiDGE uses non-uniform sampling over robot/block configurations to place blocks as bridges between disconnected support surfaces and proves probabilistic completeness. It is not obstacle clearing, but it is the strongest current evidence for the broader framing “learn or design search bias for actions that create connectivity.”
- `namosim` · read `unread` · role `baseline` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Brown, Saraydaryan, Renault, Simonin — NAMOSIM: a Robot Motion Planner for Navigation Among Movable Obstacles** (JOSS 10(113):8816, 2025). An open ROS 2/Python NAMO planner and simulator for 2D polygonal environments. It is potentially the cheapest executable external planning reference; test compatibility before promising it as a quantitative baseline because its grasp/relocate manipulation abstraction is not our push controller.

### Modern learned interactive-navigation neighbours

- `wang-affordance-namo` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Wang, Luo, Onol, Padir — Affordance-Based Mobile Robot Navigation Among Movable Obstacles** (IROS 2020; arXiv:2102.04918). Extracts obstacle movability affordances and uses contact-implicit trajectory optimization, with Toyota HSR hardware experiments. - `swipebot` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Zherdev, Kurenkov, Belikova, Tsetserukou — SwipeBot: DNN-Based Autonomous Robot Navigation Among Movable Obstacles in Cluttered Environments** (arXiv:2305.04851, 2023). It is a learned reactive baseline concept, but does not expose a setup/finish search tree or optimize simulator calls.
- `in-sight` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Schoch, Yang, Ma, Leutenegger, Hutter, Leboutet — IN-Sight: Interactive Navigation Through Sight** (IROS 2024, pp. 7794–7800; arXiv:2408.00343). Learns pixel-wise traversability and a local path planner from RGB-D, integrates predictions into a map, and transfers to ANYmal. It treats interaction as learned traversability rather than explicit object-level keyhole search, making it important broader interactive-navigation context but not a direct region-opening baseline.
- `hero-namo` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Wang, Feng, Fang, Zhang, Jing, Li, Jiang, Xu — HERO: Hierarchical Traversable 3D Scene Graphs for Embodied Navigation Among Movable Obstacles** (arXiv:2512.15047, 2025). Represents operable obstacles as potential pathways in a hierarchical 3D scene graph and reasons about them during long-horizon embodied navigation. It is relevant to the higher-level question of which blocked connection to open; it does not solve our local ordering problem over executable pushes.
- `coins` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Zhou et al. — CoINS: Counterfactual Interactive Navigation via Skill-Aware VLM** (arXiv:2601.03956, 2026). Fine-tunes a skill-aware VLM to reason about how object removal changes navigation connectivity, then executes decisions through learned low-level interaction skills. It is important current evidence that connectivity creation has become a learned reasoning target, but its high-level object-removal decision and end-to-end benchmark are not equivalent to ranking verified one-object push attempts.
- `bi-legged-pushing` · read `unread` · role `context` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Bi, Chen, Zheng, Li, Li, Ma — Interactive Navigation for Legged Manipulators With Learned Arm-Pushing Controller** (arXiv:2503.01474, 2025). Uses a two-stage RL controller to reach feasible contact zones and maintain stable arm pushing in tight spaces, with real-robot experiments. Relevant to controller learning and embodiment, not to ranking multiple verified high-level pushes.
- `yang-namo-hrl` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Yang, Hwang, Jeong, Yoon, Yoon — Efficient Navigation Among Movable Obstacles Using a Mobile Manipulator via Hierarchical Policy Learning** (IROS 2025; arXiv:2506.15380). A high-level policy outputs contact position, contact-face angle, and base velocity while a low-level whole-body policy executes the push and estimates mass, friction, and center of mass from interaction. It evaluates end-to-end success, path length, and completion time; compare framing and action abstraction, not canonical local search numbers.

      Rearrangement** (RSS 2015). Own lab. Non-monotone rearrangement: instances needing an object
      grasped more than once. Prehensile, so the contrast with pushing is the point.
- `bench-push` · read `unread` · role `must-cite` · verification `metadata-checked` — **Zhong et al. — Bench-Push: Benchmarking Pushing-based Navigation and Manipulation Tasks for Mobile Robots** (CRV 2026, arXiv:2512.11736). Open-source Pymunk/MuJoCo benchmark with four tasks: Maze and Ship-Ice navigation, plus Box-Delivery and Area-Clearing manipulation. Maze repeatedly contains local movable-obstacle passage problems related to our keyhole, while Box-Delivery and Area-Clearing use a TurtleBot3 Burger with a front bumper and introduce multi-object ordering and routing. Its standard interface uses continuous heading/angular-velocity control rather than ranked push primitives, making it most useful as an external generalization benchmark rather than a direct canonical comparison. The earlier Bench-NPIN preprint (arXiv:2505.12084) was withdrawn and explicitly superseded by this paper; do not list or cite it separately.

### Notes on the sweep itself

The audit found no fabricated papers, but four defects worth knowing: HardSATGEN's "hardness
collapse" was a **quoted phrase that appears nowhere in that paper**; a HardCore runtime figure
could not be sourced; NeSIG's speedup was reported as 6.8× against a paper that says 15.5×; and
G2SAT was credited with preserving *relative* solver behaviour, which it does not claim. Two
arXiv ids were wrong (corrected above). Treat any number from a sweep as unverified until you've
seen it in the PDF.

`docs/papers/` holds 58 PDFs from the earlier `research_compass.md` line (TAMP, diffusion, world
models, grasping). Only `05_tamp_search_guidance/` overlaps with this list.
